# Individual Contributor License Agreement — Aisotropy

> This document has **not been reviewed by counsel**. It exists so that the review has a concrete

## 1. Definitions

**"You"** (or **"Your"**) shall mean the copyright owner or legal entity authorized by the copyright
owner that is making this Agreement with the Company. For legal entities, the entity making a
Contribution and all other entities that control, are controlled by, or are under common control with
that entity are considered to be a single Contributor. For the purposes of this definition, "control"
means (i) the power, direct or indirect, to cause the direction or management of such entity, whether
by contract or otherwise, or (ii) ownership of fifty percent (50%) or more of the outstanding shares,
or (iii) beneficial ownership of such entity.

**"Contribution"** shall mean any original work of authorship, including any modifications or additions
to an existing work, that is intentionally submitted by You to the Company for inclusion in, or
documentation of, any of the products owned or managed by the Company (the "Work"). For the purposes of
this definition, "submitted" means any form of electronic, verbal, or written communication sent to the
Company or its representatives, including but not limited to communication on electronic mailing lists,
source code control systems, and issue tracking systems that are managed by, or on behalf of, the
Company for the purpose of discussing and improving the Work, but excluding communication that is
conspicuously marked or otherwise designated in writing by You as "Not a Contribution."

## 2. Grant of Copyright License

Subject to the terms and conditions of this Agreement, You hereby grant to the Company and to recipients
of software distributed by the Company a perpetual, worldwide, non-exclusive, no-charge, royalty-free,
irrevocable copyright license to reproduce, prepare derivative works of, publicly display, publicly
perform, **sublicense**, and distribute Your Contributions and such derivative works.

> **Plain-language note (not part of the Agreement).** The word *sublicense* is the one that matters and
> the one you should weigh before signing. It permits the Company to offer Your Contribution under terms
> other than Apache-2.0 — including as part of a paid or proprietary offering. §9's Outbound Commitment
> is the counterweight: whatever else the Company does, it cannot *stop* making Your Contribution
> available as open source. If that trade is not acceptable to you, do not sign — open an issue instead
> and the change can be discussed on other terms.

## 8. Notify us if this stops being true

## 9. The Company's Outbound Commitment

later version of that license, or under another license approved by the Open Source Initiative. This
undertaking survives any change of control of the Company and any change to the Project's licensing for
other purposes. **It does not restrict the Company's rights under §2** — the Company may additionally
offer the Work, including Your Contribution, under other terms.

## Deviations from the Apache ICLA v2.2 (every one, none silent)

| # | Apache ICLA v2.2 | Here | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Recipient is "The Apache Software Foundation (the 'Foundation')" | Recipient is **Luum** (the "Company"), for the Aisotropy project | The recipient is a company, not a foundation. |
| 2 | *"the Foundation shall not use Your Contributions in a way that is contrary to the public benefit or inconsistent with its nonprofit status and bylaws"* | **Replaced** by the Outbound Commitment (§9) | **The substantive change.** The ASF clause is the contributor's reciprocal protection and is anchored in nonprofit status Luum does not have; copying it verbatim would be inapplicable at best and misleading at worst. Deleting it outright would leave the contributor with a bare grant and nothing in return — the pattern that makes company CLAs unpopular, and fairly so. §9 substitutes a promise a company *can* actually make and be held to. **This is the #1 question for counsel** ([packet](counsel-review-packet.md) Q2), and the #1 decision for the operator: the alternative is to drop §9, which is more permissive for the Company and materially less attractive to contributors. |
| 3 | Filing mechanics: e-mail a signed PDF to `secretary@apache.org`; "preferred Apache id(s)"; "notify project"; link to the ASF privacy policy | Removed; replaced by the GitHub-user field, the retention paragraph in §10, and the process in [`README.md`](README.md) | Apache-specific infrastructure that does not exist here. |
| 4 | §2 grant | **Unchanged, including `sublicense`** | This is the operative clause for [ADR-0080](../decisions/0080-project-license-apache-2.md)'s stated purpose. Recorded explicitly so nobody later assumes it was softened. |
| 5 | §§1, 3–8 | Unchanged in substance; headings added, wording of the employer cross-reference points at this repo's corporate CLA | Formatting and cross-references only. |
| 6 | — | Plain-language note under §2 | Not part of the Agreement. A CLA that a contributor signs without understanding the sublicense right is a CLA that generates resentment later. |
| 6b | Preamble: *"It does not change your rights to use your own Contributions for any other purpose."* | Same sentence, **extended**: *"— you keep your copyright, and you may license the same work to anyone else, including yourself, on any terms you like."* Also *"its users"* → *"the Project's users"*. | Restates the ASF sentence's consequence in plain terms; adds no obligation on either side. **Listed because it is inside the operative text rather than in a marked note** — found by an independent re-read against the source PDF on 2026-08-03, after the table already claimed no deviation was silent. Counsel should confirm the gloss is an accurate statement of what §2 leaves the contributor, or strike it: the ASF sentence alone is sufficient. |
| 7 | No governing-law or venue clause | Still none | Deliberately left for counsel rather than guessed ([packet](counsel-review-packet.md) Q3). |
